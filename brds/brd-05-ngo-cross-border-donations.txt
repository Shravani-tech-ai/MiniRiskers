BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (BRD)

1. Change Overview
Title: Cross-Border Donation Collection Account for FCRA-Registered NGOs
Change Type: New Product
Requested By: Business Byer
Business Unit: Institutional and NGO Banking
Summary: Launch of a specialised current account product for FCRA-
registered non-profits (Section 8 companies, charitable trusts, and
societies) to receive cross-border donations from foreign donors
primarily in the US, UK, EU, and UAE. The account is designed to
operate within the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010 as
amended in 2020 (FCRA Amendment Act 2020), which mandated that all
foreign contributions be received only in the FCRA-designated bank
account at a notified branch (State Bank of India until 1 July 2024;
the bank is now eligible under the FCRA Amendment Rules 2024 to be
notified). Donations are pooled in INR after FX conversion. No outward
remittance is enabled from this account.

2. Product Description
Product Name: NGO Cross-Border Donation Account (FCRA)
Product Category: Institutional Banking / Non-Profit
Description: Designated FCRA current account under FCRA 2010 read with
the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Rules 2024. Receives
foreign donations via SWIFT and through partner correspondent banks.
Includes quarterly Statement of Receipts and Source (SRS) and
intimation to MHA via the FCRA online portal. The bank's responsibility
is limited to receipt, FX conversion, and statutory record keeping;
utilisation is monitored separately by MHA.
Transaction Type: Inbound cross-border donation / FCRA receipt
Currency: INR, USD, GBP, EUR, AED, CAD, AUD
Countries Supported: India plus USA, UK, EU member states, UAE, Canada,
Australia
Per-Transaction Limit: 10000000 (per donation receipt; cumulative
subject to FCRA limits)
Expected Monthly Volume: 1500
New Product Flag: Yes

8. Additional Notes
This BRD is a HIGH-risk scenario driven primarily by the customer-
segment risk dimension. NGO / non-profit customers are classified as
high-risk by RBI KYC Master Direction (Section on Enhanced Due
Diligence for non-profit organisations). PEP exposure is set because
several trustees of large Indian NGOs are politically exposed. The
account is heavily regulated under FCRA 2010 (as amended in 2020) and
the FCRA Amendment Rules 2024. Use this BRD to confirm that the
customer risk score dominates the calculation even when there is no
high-risk jurisdiction and no third-party issue. Regulatory evidence
should surface PMLA 2002 obligations on reporting entities, FIU-IND
reporting material, the RBI KYC Master Direction section on NGOs, and
the FCRA 2010 with its 2020 and 2024 amendments. Expected band: HIGH.
