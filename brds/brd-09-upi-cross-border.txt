BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (BRD)

1. Change Overview
Title: UPI Cross-Border Expansion to UAE, Singapore, France, and Sri Lanka
Change Type: Change to Existing Product
Requested By: Business Owner
Business Unit: Digital Payments and International Banking
Summary: Enable UPI acceptance and outbound payments in four
international corridors that have gone live with bilateral UPI
arrangements in 2024-2025: UAE (live February 2024), Sri Lanka (live
November 2024), Singapore (live September 2024), and France (live
February 2025). NPCI has signed bilateral MoUs with each country's
national payment switch (Maldives, Mauritius, and Bahrain are also in
the pipeline). The bank's role is to enable its issued debit cards and
UPI apps to interoperate with these international switches, and to
enable inbound acceptance for foreign tourists transiting through
Indian merchants. This BRD is being raised at a moment when the RBI
and NPCI are actively marketing UPI as India's official cross-border
payments diplomacy instrument.

2. Product Description
Product Name: UPI Cross-Border (UAE / Singapore / France / Sri Lanka)
Product Category: Cross-Border Payments / Digital Payments
Description: Extension of the bank's existing UPI offering to four
bilateral cross-border corridors. Outbound: Indian UPI customers can
pay at QR merchants in the partner country via NPCI's bilateral
interoperability. Inbound: Foreign tourists with partner-country UPI
apps can pay at Indian merchants. Settlement happens in INR via the
partner country's national switch (Network International for UAE,
PayNow for Singapore, etc.) and is converted to local currency on the
other side. No FX is taken by the bank; the partner country handles
FX at its end.
Transaction Type: UPI cross-border P2M (inbound and outbound)
Currency: INR, AED, SGD, EUR, LKR
Countries Supported: India plus UAE, Singapore, France, Sri Lanka
Per-Transaction Limit: 100000 (NPCI cap for cross-border UPI)
Expected Monthly Volume: 75000
New Product Flag: No

8. Additional Notes
This is one of the topmost real-time scenarios Indian banks are
dealing with in 2025. UPI cross-border is RBI's flagship soft-power
and digital-public-infrastructure initiative. Expected risk band: MEDIUM
to HIGH depending on velocity treatment. The risk engine should
recognise the cross-border nature and elevated velocity (3) but should
NOT trigger any risk-floor rule because no jurisdiction is high-risk
and no third-party DD issue exists. Regulatory evidence should
reference RBI's UPI circulars, NPCI's bilateral MoU announcements, the
FEMA framework for cross-border INR settlement, and RBI's Payment and
Settlement Systems Act 2007. Use this BRD to verify the system
correctly classifies a "change to existing product" (not new product)
while still flagging the velocity and cross-border dimensions.
